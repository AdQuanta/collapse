# Scientific Writing & Communication

This hub documents the standards for translating research results into high-impact scientific publications (e.g., PRL, Nature Physics). The goal is to produce the strongest scientifically defensible story supported by available evidence.

## The Scientific Story Model
A high-impact paper is structured to answer six linked questions:
1. **Territory**: What important scientific problem is at stake?
2. **Tension**: What standard assumption or limitation blocks progress?
3. **Advance**: What do we show, demonstrate, or derive?
4. **Mechanism**: Which physical principle makes the advance possible?
5. **Proof**: What decisive evidence distinguishes the claim from alternatives?
6. **Horizon**: What broader physics becomes accessible because of this mechanism?

## The Integrity Boundary
To maintain scientific rigor, a strict distinction is made between different evidence states:
`measured | simulated | analytically derived | literature-established | inferred | hypothesized | projected`

### Fundamental Rules:
- **No Invention**: Never "complete" missing science or fabricate parameters/citations.
- **Wording Ceilings**: Use the strongest verb justified by the evidence (e.g., `is consistent with` $\to$ `suggests` $\to$ `supports` $\to$ `demonstrates` $\to$ `establishes`).
- **Separation**: Keep mechanism, evidence, caveats, and significance distinct.

## Drafting Workflow
The project follows a **Figure-First** architecture:
1. **Pitch**: Define the one-sentence core narrative.
2. **Claim Ladder**: Map the sequence of 3–7 major claims and their evidence support.
3. **Figure Design**: Design the figures before writing prose; the figures should form a coherent argument.
4. **Prose Construction**: Draft the results around the figures, then the introduction and discussion.
