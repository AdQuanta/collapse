# Reviewer Response Protocol

Responding to peer review requires absolute scientific exactness paired with an open, generous tone. When a reviewer misunderstands a result, treat the manuscript's presentation as part of the problem even when the underlying mathematical derivation or numerical evidence is flawless.

---

## 1. The Core Response Loop

For every referee comment, complete the five-step loop:
$$\text{Acknowledge} \longrightarrow \text{Answer Scientifically} \longrightarrow \text{Make / Justify Action} \longrightarrow \text{Identify Modified Text/Figure} \longrightarrow \text{Cite Evidence}$$

1. **Acknowledge**: Thank the referee for the specific observation, summarizing their concern accurately without distortion or minimization.
2. **Answer Scientifically**: Provide a self-contained theoretical or numerical response directly in the reply letter, addressing the physics head-on.
3. **Make / Justify Action**: Detail the concrete modification made to the manuscript or Supplemental Material (or provide the scientific justification if no textual change is warranted).
4. **Identify Modified Text/Figure**: Explicitly cite the page, section, equation, or figure number, quoting the exact revised passage in a distinct blockquote.
5. **Cite Evidence**: Reference specific datasets, proof steps, controls, or published literature (hyperlinked via DOI).

---

## 2. Resolving Disagreements Scientifically

When a referee raises an objection that conflicts with verified theorems or data:
$$\text{Appreciation} \longrightarrow \text{Shared Ground} \longrightarrow \text{Evidence / Reasoning} \longrightarrow \text{Narrow Disagreement} \longrightarrow \text{Manuscript Clarification}$$

- **Appreciation**: Validate the importance of the physical question raised.
- **Shared Ground**: State clearly where the referee's intuition is correct (e.g., agreeing that generic Haar unitaries do not yield a preferred measurement axis).
- **Evidence / Reasoning**: Present the decisive evidence that distinguishes the current system from the referee's default assumption (e.g., pointing out that structured many-body spin rings have non-trivial spectral correlations absent in Haar ensembles).
- **Narrow Disagreement**: Isolate the exact point of difference to avoid broad rhetorical debates.
- **Manuscript Clarification**: Revise the manuscript text to ensure future readers do not encounter the same ambiguity.

---

## 3. Stylistic & Professional Standards

- **Tone**: Generous, objective, and rigorously technical. Avoid defensive or dismissive language (e.g., never write "the referee failed to understand").
- **Substantive Transparency**: Never describe a minor grammatical tweak in a way that attempts to minimize a deep conceptual objection. If a referee identified a genuine gap in controls, run the control or add the appropriate caveat to the main text.
- **Terminology Consistency**: Use one term consistently throughout the response document—either `Reviewer` or `Referee`, never mixing them arbitrarily.
- **DOI Hyperlinks**: Hyperlink every cited external paper by its digital object identifier (DOI) directly in the response text so the editor and referee can verify citations with a single click.
- **Self-Sufficiency**: The response document should be fully readable without requiring the referee to constantly cross-reference multiple files simultaneously. Quote revised sentences or paragraphs in full.
