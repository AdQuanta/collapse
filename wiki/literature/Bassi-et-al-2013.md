# Bassi et al. 2013 — Models of wave-function collapse, underlying theories, and experimental tests

**Reference**: A. Bassi, K. Lochan, S. Satin, T. P. Singh, and H. Ulbricht, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **85**, 471 (2013).

## Core Thesis
The transition from quantum to classical behavior is driven by fundamental, stochastic modifications to the Schrödinger equation. These "collapse models" (such as GRW and CSL) provide a unified framework that explains both microscopic quantum behavior and macroscopic classicality without needing to invoke an external observer or a "Heisenberg cut."

## Key Contributions
- **Unified Theory of Collapse**: Synthesizes various dynamical reduction models, including the GRW (discrete jumps) and CSL (continuous stochastic evolution) models.
- **Connection to Gravity**: Reviews theories (Penrose, Diòsi) that propose gravitational energy uncertainty as the trigger for wave-function collapse.
- **Trace Dynamics**: Explores the possibility that quantum mechanics emerges as a thermodynamic limit of a more fundamental theory of non-commuting matrices (Adler).
- **Experimental Roadmap**: Outlines specific, falsifiable tests for collapse models, including matter-wave interferometry with massive molecules and the search for spontaneous radiation from "kicked" charged particles.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
Bassi et al. present the state-of-the-art in **non-unitary** explanations of the measurement problem.

The `unitary-collapse` project positions itself as the **unitary alternative** to this entire class of models. While Bassi et al. describe the search for an "external" source of stochasticity (like gravitational noise or a fundamental collapse rate $\lambda$), the `unitary-collapse` project argues that the "collapse" is an **internal** property of the system's dynamics. 

By showing that the distribution of collapsible states follows the Born rule, the project demonstrates that a "classical outcome" can be achieved using the *same* linear evolution that Bassi et al. view as the problem to be solved. The project effectively argues that the "stochasticity" seen in collapse models is an effective description of the high-dimensional, unitary evolution of a structured detector.
