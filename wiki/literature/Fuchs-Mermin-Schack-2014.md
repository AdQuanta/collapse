# Fuchs, Mermin & Schack 2014 — An Introduction to QBism

**Reference**: Christopher A. Fuchs, N. David Mermin, and Rüdiger Schack, "An introduction to QBism with an application to the measurement problem", *American Journal of Physics* **82**(8), 749–754 (2014). DOI: [10.1119/1.4874812](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4874812). arXiv: [1311.5253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5253).

## Core Thesis
The canonical, highly accessible presentation of QBism (Quantum Bayesianism). The authors demonstrate that once a quantum state is recognized not as an objective physical entity, but as an agent's personal degree of belief (credence) about future measurement experiences, the long-standing paradoxes of quantum foundations—the measurement problem, Wigner's friend, and Bell nonlocality—dissolve immediately without requiring any modifications to quantum mechanics or hidden variables.

## Key Arguments & Conceptual Insights
- **The Measurement Problem Dissolved:** Measurement is an action an agent takes on the world, and the outcome is the personal experience elicited in that agent. "Collapse" is simply the agent updating their personal belief state $\rho \to \rho'$ via Bayesian conditionalization. No physical reduction occurs in spacetime.
- **Wigner's Friend Paradox:** The Friend in an isolated lab records an outcome and updates their state to $|0\rangle$; Wigner outside assigns an entangled state $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle|F_0\rangle + |1\rangle|F_1\rangle)$. Both descriptions are correct because quantum states are personal to the evaluating agent.
- **Locality Restored:** In an EPR-Bohm experiment, Alice's measurement on particle A does not physically change Bob's particle B across spacelike intervals; it only updates Alice's expectations regarding what she will experience when she later interacts with Bob.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
QBism represents the ultimate $\psi$-epistemic resolution of the measurement problem: collapse is dissolved by denying that states are physically real. In contrast, `unitary-collapse` represents a strictly $\psi$-ontic program: states are real physical entities, and collapse is a physical, deterministic transit driven by the detector Hamiltonian acting on projective roots. The two approaches occupy opposite poles of the epistemic/ontic divide.
