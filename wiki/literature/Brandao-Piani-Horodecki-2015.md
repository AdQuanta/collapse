# Brandão, Piani, Horodecki 2015 — Generic emergence of classical features in quantum Darwinism

**Reference**: F. Brandão, M. Piani, and P. Horodecki, *Nat. Commun.* **6**, 8908 (2015).

## Core Thesis
The emergence of classical objectivity (specifically, the fact that multiple observers agree on the same pointer observable) is a **generic property** of quantum mechanics, rooted in the monogamy of entanglement, rather than a specific feature of particular interaction Hamiltonians.

## Key Contributions
- **Genericity of the Pointer Observable**: Proves that it is mathematically impossible for many independent observers to each hold different quantum information about a single system. Therefore, any redundantly shared information must necessarily be classical information about one specific "pointer" observable.
- **Distinction between Observable and Outcome**: 
    - **Objectivity of Observables** (Which thing is being measured?): Proved to be **generic**.
    - **Objectivity of Outcomes** (What is the result?): Proved to be **non-generic**, depending on the specific dynamics of the interaction.
- **Operational Discord**: Provides an operational interpretation of quantum discord as the information lost when attempting to broadcast quantum correlations redundantly.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
`literature-established`: This paper suggests that "pointer observables" are an inevitable consequence of entanglement distribution. 

However, the `unitary-collapse` project focuses on the **objectivity of outcomes** (the second point above), which Brandão et al. identify as **non-generic**. This justifies the project's search for *specific structural conditions* on the Hamiltonian. If the emergence of a definite outcome (the "collapse") is non-generic, then identifying the exact class of Hamiltonians that *do* produce a Born-like distribution of outcomes is a non-trivial and scientifically valuable task.
