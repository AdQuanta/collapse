# Dürr, Goldstein & Zanghì 1992 — Quantum Equilibrium and the Origin of Absolute Uncertainty

**Reference**: D. Dürr, S. Goldstein, and N. Zanghì, *Journal of Statistical Physics* **67**(5–6), 843–907 (1992). DOI: [10.1007/BF01049004](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01049004). arXiv: [quant-ph/0308039](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308039).

**Companion review**: D. Dürr, S. Goldstein, and N. Zanghì, *Journal of Statistical Physics* **116**(1–4), 959–1055 (2004). arXiv: [quant-ph/0308038](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308038).

## Core Contributions
- **First-order reformulation:** Discarded Bohm's second-order quantum-potential picture as misleading. The fundamental law is the guiding equation: $\dot{\mathbf{Q}}_k = (\hbar/m_k) \operatorname{Im}(\nabla_k \Psi / \Psi)$.
- **Equivariance theorem:** If $\rho(t_0) = |\Psi(t_0)|^2$, then $\rho(t) = |\Psi(t)|^2$ for all $t$, because the Liouville and Schrödinger continuity equations coincide under the guiding flow.
- **Typicality of Born's rule:** For almost all initial universal configurations $Q_0$ (measured by $|\Psi|^2 dQ_0$), the empirical distribution of subsystem outcomes converges to Born's conditional distribution—analogous to the microcanonical derivation of Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics.
- **Conditional wave function:** Defined $\psi_{\text{cond}}(x, t) = \Psi(x, Y(t), t)$ (conditioned on actual environment configuration $Y(t)$), deriving textbook operator formalism and POVMs without operators being fundamental.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
Both DGZ and `unitary-collapse` treat Born's rule as **emergent** rather than axiomatic. DGZ derives it from typicality under the equivariant measure $|\Psi|^2$; `unitary-collapse` derives it from the geometric distribution of projective roots in the thermodynamic limit. The two programs represent distinct strategies for the same foundational goal.
