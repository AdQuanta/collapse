# Schlosshauer — Decoherence, the measurement problem, and interpretations of quantum mechanics

**Reference**: M. Schlosshauer, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **76**, 1267 (2005).

## Core Thesis
Decoherence provides the physical mechanism for the quantum-to-classical transition by explaining how the environment "monitors" a system, thereby resolving the preferred-basis problem. While it doesn't solve the "problem of definite outcomes" (the transition from an improper mixture to a single reality), it renders quantum interference practically invisible on macroscopic scales.

## Key Contributions
- **The Two-Fold Measurement Problem**: Distinguishes between the **preferred-basis problem** (solved by einselection) and the **problem of definite outcomes** (which requires an interpretation).
- **Improper vs. Proper Mixtures**: A crucial formal distinction. Decoherence produces an **improper mixture** (reduced density matrix) which looks like a classical ensemble but remains a global pure state. This highlights that decoherence alone is not "collapse."
- **Interpretation Bridge**: Analyzes how decoherence informs various interpretations of QM, from Many-Worlds (where it drives branching) to Copenhagen (where it motivates the Heisenberg cut).
- **Einselection Synthesis**: Synthesizes the "pointer state" concept, showing that the interaction Hamiltonian $H_{int}$ determines the robustness of specific states against environmental noise.
