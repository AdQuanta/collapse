# GRW 1986 — A Theory of Spontaneous Localization

**Reference**: G. C. Ghirardi, A. Rimini, and T. Weber, *Phys. Rev. A* **34**, 470 (1986).

## Core Thesis
The "collapse of the wave function" is not a consequence of measurement or observers, but a fundamental, random physical process. Every particle in the universe is subject to spontaneous "hits" (localizations) that shrink the wave function. For macroscopic objects, these hits occur so frequently that any superposition is suppressed almost instantaneously.

## Key Mechanisms
- **Spontaneous Localization**: A particle's wave function is randomly multiplied by a Gaussian function, localizing the particle in space.
- **The Amplification Mechanism**: In a macro-object of $N$ particles, a single hit on any particle collapses the entire entangled state. The effective collapse rate $\Lambda = N\lambda$ is huge for macroscopic objects, while remaining negligible for single atoms.
- **Unified Dynamics**: Replaces the two-rule structure of QM (Schrödinger evolution vs. reduction postulate) with a single, stochastic dynamical law.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
GRW and the `unitary-collapse` project both seek to eliminate the "observer" from the measurement process, but they do so from opposite directions:
- **GRW (Non-Unitary)**: Adds a stochastic, non-unitary term to the Schrödinger equation to *force* collapse.
- **`unitary-collapse` (Unitary)**: Seeks to *derive* collapse-like outcomes from purely unitary evolution of a specific system structure.

While GRW argues that we must modify the laws of physics to get definite outcomes, the `unitary-collapse` project argues that **the laws of physics (unitarity) are sufficient**, provided we identify the correct initial "collapsible states" and detector Hamiltonians. The project effectively tries to "mimic" the results of GRW (definite outcomes) using only the tools of standard unitary quantum mechanics.
