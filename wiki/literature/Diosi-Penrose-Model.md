# Diósi-Penrose Model — Gravity-Induced Collapse

**References**: 
- L. Diósi, "Gravitational-induced decoherence" (1987/1989).
- R. Penrose, "On Gravity's Role in Quantum Collapse" (1996).

## Core Thesis
Gravity is the physical agent responsible for the collapse of the quantum wave function. A spatial superposition of a massive object creates a superposition of two different space-time curvatures; this configuration is inherently unstable and must decay into a single definite state.

## Comparison of Approaches
- **Lajos Diósi (Stochastic Noise)**: Views collapse as the result of the system interacting with universal, stochastic gravitational fluctuations. He provided a master equation describing the decoherence of the density matrix.
- **Roger Penrose (Geometric Instability)**: Views collapse as a conflict between the principle of superposition and General Relativity. He estimates the collapse time $\tau \approx \hbar / \Delta E_G$, where $\Delta E_G$ is the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the superposed mass distributions.

## Common Result: The Collapse Rate
Both models converge on the same fundamental prediction: the collapse rate is proportional to the gravitational energy difference between the superposed states. 
- **Microscopic systems** have negligible mass and thus negligible gravitational effects $\to$ they remain quantum.
- **Macroscopic systems** have high mass density $\to$ they collapse almost instantaneously.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
The Diósi-Penrose model is the prime example of " modifying the laws of physics" (replacing the Schrödinger equation with a non-unitary one) to explain measurement.

The `unitary-collapse` project's objective is to achieve the **same outcome** (the suppression of macroscopic superpositions and the emergence of definite states) while **retaining the original laws of physics** (unitarity). 

By identifying a "Born-like" distribution of collapsible states, the project suggests that the "collapse" is not a failure of the Schrödinger equation caused by gravity, but an emergent property of the **unitary evolution of a sufficiently complex detector**. The project effectively proposes a "unitary substitute" for the Diósi-Penrose mechanism.
