# Donadi & Hossenfelder 2022 — Testing Superdeterminism

**Reference**:
- S. Donadi and S. Hossenfelder, *Physical Review A* **106**(2), 022212 (2022). arXiv: [2203.02335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02335).
- S. Donadi and S. Hossenfelder, *Physical Review A* **104**(5), 052225 (2021). (Toy model for local deterministic collapse.)
- S. Donadi and S. Hossenfelder, *Foundations of Physics* (2024). arXiv: [2308.12789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12789). (Cold atom / quantum memory protocols.)

## Core Contribution
Proposes concrete experimental protocols to test whether superdeterministic correlations have a **finite memory timescale**: if hidden variables must equilibrate with detector settings through microscopic interactions, rapidly repeated measurements (faster than re-thermalization) should reveal deviations from standard QM predictions.

## Key Idea
In superdeterministic theories, the correlation $P(\lambda \mid a, b) \neq P(\lambda)$ must be established through physical interactions. If measurements are repeated faster than the hidden variables can adjust, the correlations should be incomplete, producing measurable deviations from quantum predictions—particularly in quantum memory / delayed-choice configurations.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
If projective roots are the physical mechanism for state restriction, the timescale over which the detector "selects" the appropriate root is experimentally relevant. Donadi-Hossenfelder's protocols could, in principle, probe whether the root selection has a finite dynamical timescale.
