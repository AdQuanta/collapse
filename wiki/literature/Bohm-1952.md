# Bohm 1952 — A Suggested Interpretation of the Quantum Theory in Terms of "Hidden" Variables

**Reference**:
- D. Bohm, *Physical Review* **85**(2), 166–179 (1952). Part I. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRev.85.166](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.166).
- D. Bohm, *Physical Review* **85**(2), 180–193 (1952). Part II. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRev.85.180](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.180).

## Core Thesis
Quantum mechanics admits a deterministic, causal reinterpretation in which particles possess definite positions at all times, guided by a pilot wave $\Psi$ through a quantum potential $Q = -(\hbar^2/2m)(\nabla^2 R / R)$. Born's rule $\rho = |\Psi|^2$ is an equilibrium distribution analogous to thermal equilibrium, not a fundamental axiom.

## Key Contributions
- **Part I:** Derived equations of motion from the polar decomposition of Schrödinger's equation. Resolved Pauli's 1927 objection to de Broglie's pilot wave concerning inelastic scattering. Provided exact trajectory solutions for stationary states, tunneling, and two-slit interference.
- **Part II:** Solved the quantum measurement problem dynamically. Showed that apparatus–system entanglement creates separated wave packets in configuration space; the particle enters exactly one packet, producing effective collapse without non-unitary dynamics.

## Subsequent Developments
- **DGZ (1992):** Reformulated as first-order velocity dynamics; equivariance and typicality replace the quantum potential view.
- **Valentini (1991, 2005):** $|\Psi|^2$ as contingent equilibrium; non-equilibrium allows superluminal signaling.
- **Bell (1964):** Discovered his inequality by asking whether Bohm's nonlocality is necessary.
- Standard textbooks: Holland (1993), Dürr & Teufel (2009).

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
Bohmian mechanics and `unitary-collapse` are **orthogonal** approaches to the measurement problem. Bohm keeps all states accessible and adds nonlocal hidden variables; `unitary-collapse` restricts states to projective roots within standard unitary QM. Both derive Born's rule from structure rather than postulating it.
