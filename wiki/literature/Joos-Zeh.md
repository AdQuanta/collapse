# Joos & Zeh — The Emergence of Classical Properties Through Interaction with the Environment

**Reference**: E. Joos and H. D. Zeh, *Z. Phys. B* **69**, 617–633 (1985).

## Core Thesis
Classicality is an emergent property of quantum systems interacting with their environment. The environment continuously "measures" the system, effectively destroying quantum superpositions and selecting a preferred basis (usually position).

## Key Mechanisms
- **Environmental Decoherence**: Interaction with the surroundings (e.g., scattering of CMB photons) entangles the system with the environment, causing the off-diagonal elements of the reduced density matrix to vanish.
- **Localization**: For macroscopic objects, coherence lengths shrink almost instantaneously, explaining why we observe definite positions rather than spatial superpositions.
- **The "Heisenberg Cut" as an Emergent Boundary**: Argues that the boundary between the quantum and classical worlds is a dynamic result of the rate of decoherence.

## Relevance to `unitary-collapse`
`literature-established`: Joos and Zeh treat the environment as an infinite, uncontrollable sink of information that enforces classicality. 

The `unitary-collapse` project investigates the **finite-system limit** of this process. If decoherence is the "limit" where the environment is huge, can a small, structured detector (a finite-dimensional "environment") mimic this behavior? The project seeks the "minimum requirements" for this emergence—identifying specific unitary dynamics where a finite detector can produce the same "definite outcome" statistics that Joos and Zeh attribute to an infinite environment.
