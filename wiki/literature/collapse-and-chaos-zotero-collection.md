This collection contains 28 distinct sources in nine subcollections. DOI normalization and normalized title/year matching found no duplicates. Similarly named Barandes and Schulman records are separate works.

**Evidence depth:** Complete bibliographic metadata is available for all 28 records and Zotero abstracts for 14. Every record has a PDF attachment entry, but none of the 28 local PDF payloads is present and Zotero full-text retrieval fails. Statements below are therefore metadata- or abstract-level unless an existing independently sourced wiki page says otherwise.

## Master equations and memory

- Kolovsky (2020), “Quantum entanglement and the Born-Markov approximation for an open quantum system,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.101.062116) — `IAHS26F3`; metadata only.
- Albert and Jiang (2014), “Symmetries and conserved quantities in Lindblad master equations,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.89.022118) — `7WQVMU8V`; metadata only.
- O'Donovan et al. (2025), “Quantum master equation from the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/kh96-ct3y) — `QLNS2WBI`; the stored abstract says ETH yields a master equation for a weakly coupled system and finite chaotic pure-state bath, with Markovianity controlled by the ETH spectral function and poor finite-size agreement for an integrable bath.
- Breuer et al. (2016), *Colloquium: Non-Markovian dynamics in open quantum systems*, [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.88.021002) — `74LLM4HV`; metadata only.

These works concern reduced dynamics. They can motivate controls and language, but a Lindblad or Born-Markov approximation cannot replace the exact closed-system pencils required by `SPEC.md`.

## Spontaneous disentanglement models

- Buks (2024), “Spontaneous disentanglement and thermalisation,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2404.17580) — `J3HN8EIV`; abstract available.

## Stochastic-quantum correspondence and divisibility

- Pimenta (2025), “Divisible and indivisible Stochastic-Quantum dynamics,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.08785) — `DFJE9FP9`; abstract describes a geometric classification of two-configuration stochastic dynamics.
- Davalos, Ziman, and Pineda (2019), “Divisibility of qubit channels and dynamical maps,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2019-05-20-144) — `4H8U4QVF`; abstract available.
- Barandes (2023), “The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2309.03085) — `JUEMPZP9`; the abstract claims a correspondence between generalized stochastic systems and unitary quantum systems.
- Milz and Modi (2021), “Quantum Stochastic Processes and Quantum non-Markovian Phenomena,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.030201) — `866E4ZJZ`; metadata only.
- Wolf and Cirac (2008), “Dividing Quantum Channels,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-008-0411-y) — `EDPYGXTB`; abstract classifies divisible and indivisible channels, including qubit cases.
- Barandes (2025), “Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21192) — `I53PZPU9`; abstract available.
- Barandes (2025), “The Stochastic-Quantum Correspondence,” [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.31389/pop.186) — `AJDNMPJY`; abstract available.

These papers may sharpen the distinction between unitary representations, operational stochastic processes, and divisibility. Their conceptual correspondence does not supply the detector-state selection measure required by this project. See [[stochastic-quantum-correspondence]] and [[Barandes-2023]].
