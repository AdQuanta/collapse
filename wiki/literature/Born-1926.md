# Born 1926 — On the Quantum Mechanics of Collision Processes

**Reference**: M. Born, *Zeitschrift für Physik* **37**, 863–867 (1926).

## Core Thesis
The wave function $\psi$ is a **probability amplitude**. The probability of finding a particle in a specific state after a collision is proportional to the square of the magnitude of the wave function: $p \propto |\psi|^2$.

## Key Contributions
- **Statistical Interpretation**: Reconciled Schrödinger's wave mechanics with the discrete, particle-like outcomes of experiments (e.g., collision/scattering).
- **Indeterminism**: Rejected the notion of a causal, deterministic link between initial conditions and specific outcomes, asserting that QM provides probabilities rather than certainties.
- **The "Yield Function"**: Defined the probability of a specific collision effect as the absolute square of the amplitude of the corresponding wave.
