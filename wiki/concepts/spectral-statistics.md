# Spectral Statistics & Born Geometry

## The Wigner-Dyson (WD) Conjecture
**Hypothesis:** A detector with Wigner-Dyson level-spacing statistics (e.g., GOE) may be sufficient for Born-like root statistics.

## Findings (Finite N)
Numerical evidence from $N=17$ studies suggests that detector level statistics *alone* are not sufficient:
- Both Poisson-like and GOE-like detectors can span a wide range of Born similarity ($S_{\text{born}}$).
- There is a correlation between GOE-like statistics and Born similarity in certain families, but it is not a strict sufficiency result.

## The Haar Baseline
A global Haar-random unitary produces an isotropic distribution (non-Born). This proves that "more random" is not "more Born." The physics requires structure that preserves a preferred measurement axis while allowing mixing.

## Sector-Resolved Rule
**Mandatory:** Never mix eigenvalues from independent exact irreducible sectors before forming spacings. Mixing sectors can make a chaotic Hamiltonian look artificially Poissonian.
