# Scientific Rules (The "Never" List)

To maintain scientific integrity and prevent the over-interpretation of numerical data, the following rules are absolute.

## Numerical Integrity
- **No Trend-to-Theorem**: Never turn a numerical trend into a theorem.
- **No Scalar-Born Claims**: Never call a result "Born" merely because one scalar score (like $S_{\text{born}}$) is favorable.
- **No Pseudo-Convergence**: Do not claim convergence from a single $N$.

## Theoretical Boundaries
- **Pole Projection**: Endpoint pole projection is built into the special-state construction; it is **not** a discriminator between Born-producing and non-Born Hamiltonians.
- **Gleason/Busch**: These theorems constrain probability assignments; they do not classify Hamiltonians.
- **Chaos**: "Chaos" is not the working explanation. Indiscriminate scrambling can coexist with non-Born geometry.
- **Poisson vs. MBL**: Poisson statistics do not imply MBL. Symmetries must be resolved before level-statistics analysis.

## Physical Assumptions
- **Superposition**: The physically realized special set is generically **not** closed under superposition. Do not silently restore universal physical superposition.
- **Ontology**: Keep ontology separate from mathematics. Superselection, superdeterminism, or attracting dynamics are *interpretations*, not established mechanisms.
