# Research-group scientific writing rules

Use this reference for sentence-level and manuscript-level revisions.

## Scientific story

- Think about the pitch before line editing.
- Motivate why the central claim is interesting and worth solving.
- State the new contribution early with “Here we…” or “In this work…” when useful.
- Interleave theory and results instead of front-loading all theory.
- In the discussion, generalize, identify the most exciting implication, and end with a strong but justified outlook.

## Paragraphs

- One main topic per paragraph.
- Simple internal sentence structure.
- Optimize the order of evidence and explanation.
- Every paragraph must make clear why the reader is being told this now.

## Sentences

- Use active voice where natural.
- Prefer constructions like “X shows Y” or “Y does Z” over passive description.
- Prefer strong content verbs.
- Replace noun-heavy phrases with verbs where possible.
- Remove wordiness and redundant scaffolding.
- Split sentences longer than roughly 40 words unless the complexity is necessary.
- Ensure each sentence connects clearly to the previous and next sentence.

## Tense

Scientific reports are commonly written in present tense. Use present tense for figures, equations, general findings, and manuscript claims unless a specific completed procedure or historical event calls for past tense.

## Technical style

- Keep figure and equation reference style consistent.
- Spell out “Figure” or “Equation” when it begins a sentence.
- Use roman/upright descriptive subscripts and italic variable subscripts.
- Punctuate equations as parts of sentences.
- Put spaces between numbers and units.
- Units are upright, not italic.
- Avoid large blocks of italic text.
- Avoid prose inside equation environments when prose can remain prose.
- Avoid backslashes in prose; minimize slashes generally.

## References

- Abstracts normally omit citations unless journal rules allow them.
- The introduction should cite foundational and relevant work.
- During drafting, stable citation keys are easier than manual numbering.
- Before submission, verify all important prior work and all cited details.

## Supplementary information

- Make it stand alone.
- Redefine acronyms.
- Reintroduce essential notation.
- Use present tense by default.
- Keep the headline main-text claim supported in the main paper rather than depending critically on supplementary figures.
