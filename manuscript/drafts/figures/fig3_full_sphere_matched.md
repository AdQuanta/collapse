# Figure Card 3: Full-Sphere Matched-Ring Diagnostic

## Figure specification

1. **Claim-like title:** *The highlighted matched ring develops a strongly dipolar full-sphere root-count asymmetry.*
2. **One-sentence takeaway:** At $N=16,t=10^4$, the audited matched-field ring yields a fully occupied equal-area map whose asymmetry is dominated by a $z$-aligned dipole and has a count-weighted Born residual of $0.089$.
3. **Question answered:** Does the favorable polar score survive a two-dimensional spherical test, or is it an artifact of projecting away azimuthal structure?
4. **Panel flow:** Production root cloud plus exact antipodal partner → unsmoothed $36\times18$ equal-area asymmetry map → comparison with $a_{\mathrm B}=\mu$ → odd-multipole/free-dipole diagnostics.
5. **Dominant panel:** The full-sphere asymmetry map, which should make the dipolar gradient and absence of empty cells immediately visible.
6. **Instant comparison:** Empirical asymmetry versus the unit $z$-dipole; the Haar ensemble remains a textual or inset analytical null, not a simulated control.
7. **Quantitative anchors:** 65,536 finite roots; 648/648 bins occupied; correlation $0.971$; count-weighted residual $0.089$; $\ell=1$ fraction $0.982$ of odd power through $\ell=7$; free-fit amplitude $1.062$ and residual $0.070$.
8. **Alternative interpretation addressed:** Rules out a purely meridional or sparsely occupied cloud as the explanation for the polar agreement. It does not rule out finite-size, binning, or selection effects.
9. **Why main text:** This is the strongest post-cutoff numerical result and the only audited full-sphere map in the Letter.

## Draft caption

> **FIG. 3. Full-sphere matched-ring root-count geometry.** For the matched-field ring at $N=16$ and $t=10^4$, 65,536 finite production roots and their exact antipodal outcome partners define the equal-area asymmetry $a(\phi,\mu)$, with $\mu=\cos\theta$. The $36\times18$ grid is unsmoothed and all 648 bins are occupied. The map is strongly dipolar: its correlation with $\mu$ is $0.971$, its count-weighted residual from the unit-amplitude Born dipole is $0.089$, and $\ell=1$ carries $0.982$ of the resolved odd power through $\ell=7$. A free dipole fit gives axis $\hat{\bm n}=\hat{\bm z}$ to the displayed precision, amplitude $1.062$, and residual $0.070$. These quantities diagnose finite root counts, not measurement-shot probabilities; finer sparse grids increase the residual.
