# Abstract

## Move sequence

1. **Context and gap:** Unitary dynamics explains subsystem irreversibility but not the selection of one global measurement record.
2. **Here we:** Classify the product inputs that a finite qubit--detector unitary maps to a definite qubit pole.
3. **Exact results:** Homogeneous matrix-pencil roots, exact antipodal outcome pairing, and the Haar isotropic ensemble null.
4. **Numerical result:** One audited matched-ring sequence develops a Born-like dipolar root-count geometry.
5. **Boundary and significance:** The result defines a Hamiltonian-classification problem without claiming an operational probability law or record theorem.

## Working long draft

> Globally unitary dynamics accounts for several apparently irreversible subsystem processes, yet it does not explain how a quantum measurement yields one definite global record. We isolate a narrower question: which product inputs does a finite qubit--detector unitary map to a definite qubit pole? These inputs are homogeneous roots of a detector-space matrix pencil. For a regular detector-space pencil of dimension $d$, there are $d$ projective roots counted with algebraic multiplicity, and unitarity forces the two outcome-labelled root multisets to be exact Bloch antipodes. For Haar propagators, the roots obey the complex spherical-ensemble law with uniform one-point intensity, so the ensemble selects no preferred axis. A post-cutoff matched-field spin-ring sequence instead develops a Born-like root-count geometry: the four-time median polar score rises monotonically from $0.192$ at $N=11$ to $0.807$ at $N=16$, while the $N=16,t=10^4$ full-sphere map has unit equal-area occupancy, a count-weighted Born residual of $0.089$, and dipole-dominated odd power. These results establish an exact projective geometry and a structured-versus-Haar contrast. They do not turn algebraic multiplicity into a preparation probability or establish stable detector records.

## Polished Letter draft (129 words)

> Unitary dynamics explains several apparent subsystem arrows of time, but not how measurement selects one global record. We classify the product inputs that a finite qubit--detector unitary maps to a definite qubit pole. These inputs are projective roots of a detector-space matrix pencil, and unitarity pairs the two outcome-root multisets as exact Bloch antipodes. Haar propagators yield the complex spherical ensemble with uniform one-point intensity, so the ensemble selects no preferred axis. In one post-cutoff matched-field spin-ring sequence, the four-time median polar score rises from $0.192$ at $N=11$ to $0.807$ at $N=16$; an $N=16$ full-sphere map has unit occupancy and count-weighted Born residual $0.089$. This exact geometry and structured-versus-Haar contrast define a concrete Hamiltonian-classification problem, but root multiplicity is not a preparation probability and stable detector records remain unestablished.
