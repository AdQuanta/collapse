## 2. Working Long Draft (Completeness & Logical Rigor)

> Seemingly irreversible quantum phenomena such as spontaneous emission, environmental decoherence, and many-body thermalization are now understood as emergent subsystem manifestations of globally unitary evolution. Yet the most fundamental transition—the resolution of a quantum measurement into a single, definite outcome—is persistently treated as an external, non-unitary reduction. The core barrier is linearity: if basis inputs generate distinct records, an unrestricted superposition principle inevitably forces their linear combinations into macroscopic superpositions of detector states. Here we formulate an exact restricted-state loophole: initial product states mapped by a unitary qubit-detector propagator to a definite measurement outcome are precisely the projective roots of a detector-space matrix pencil. Unitarity and Jacobi complementary-minor duality force the two outcome-root multisets to form exact Bloch antipodes, ensuring that a regular detector of dimension $d$ produces exactly $d$ projective coordinates and their labelled partners. For Haar-random propagators, the roots form a complex spherical ensemble with uniform one-point intensity, proving that generic scrambling yields no preferred measurement axis. In contrast, a clean interacting many-body spin ring develops a pronounced Born-like dipolar root geometry: across four time decades, the median polar score rises monotonically from $0.192$ at $N=11$ to $0.807$ at $N=16$, while an $N=16$ full-sphere map achieves unit coverage and a count-weighted Born residual of $0.089$ dominated by the $\ell=1$ dipole mode ($98.2\%$). These results establish an exact algebraic boundary for measurement-compatible states and demonstrate a clean structured-versus-Haar distinction; an operational measure physically selecting these states and the mechanism of macroscopic record stabilization remain open problems.

## 3. Polished Letter Draft (PRL Format: 147 Words)

> Can quantum measurement be another apparent arrow of time emerging from globally unitary many-body dynamics? We formulate a precise restricted-state framework: initial product inputs that a finite qubit--detector unitary maps to a definite qubit pole are the projective roots of a detector-space matrix pencil. Unitarity forces the two outcome-root multisets to form exact Bloch antipodes via Jacobi complementary-minor duality, yielding $d$ projective roots and their labeled partners for a $d$-dimensional detector. For Haar propagators, the roots form a complex spherical ensemble with uniform intensity, precluding an ensemble-selected measurement axis. In a clean interacting spin ring, the root cloud instead develops a Born-like dipolar geometry: the median polar score rises monotonically from $0.192$ at $N=11$ to $0.807$ at $N=16$, while an $N=16$ full-sphere map achieves unit coverage and a Born residual of $0.089$. These results establish exact boundary-compatible states and a structured-versus-Haar distinction; a physical selection measure and stable detector records remain open.
