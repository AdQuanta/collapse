# Introduction

## Paragraph 1: Established subsystem arrows and the stronger measurement question

Spontaneous emission, environmental decoherence, and many-body thermalization show how locally irreversible behavior can emerge while the complete quantum state evolves unitarily \cite{Zurek2003,Deutsch1991,Srednicki1994}. Decoherence and quantum Darwinism further explain pointer stability and the local accessibility of classical information \cite{BrandaoPianiHorodecki2015,QiRanard2021,DoucetDeffner2024}. These mechanisms do not, by themselves, replace a global superposition of records by one term. Figure 1 therefore frames measurement as a stronger unresolved arrow: the passage from coherent alternatives to one definite global record.

## Paragraph 2: The linearity obstruction and the restricted-state question

The obstruction is linearity. If two basis inputs evolve into distinct detector records, an unrestricted superposition of those inputs evolves into the corresponding superposition of records \cite{BassiGhirardi2000}. We retain exact global unitarity and ask a narrower question: can a dynamically defined set of product boundary states reach definite qubit poles while failing to close under arbitrary addition? This restricted-state premise is adjacent to special-state approaches \cite{Schulman2012,Schulman2017}; it is a hypothesis about physically realized boundary conditions, not a derived selection law.

## Paragraph 3: Exact contribution and Haar null

Here we characterize the pole-compatible product inputs of a finite qubit--detector unitary as homogeneous roots of detector-space matrix pencils. A regular pencil of detector dimension $d$ has $d$ roots in $\mathbb{CP}^1$, counted with algebraic multiplicity, including the projective point at infinity \cite{MolerStewart1973}. Jacobi complementary-minor duality then gives a stronger same-unitary constraint: the two outcome-labelled root multisets are exact Bloch antipodes, including multiplicity. For Haar propagators, the roots obey the complex spherical-ensemble law with uniform one-point intensity \cite{Krishnapur2009,AlishahiZamani2015}. Generic Haar scrambling therefore supplies an exact isotropic ensemble null rather than a preferred measurement axis.

## Paragraph 4: Structured-ring evidence and the operational boundary

We contrast this null with one audited post-cutoff matched-field spin-ring sequence. At $N=16$ detector spins and $t=10^4$, 65,536 finite roots define a fully occupied, unsmoothed $36\times18$ equal-area map whose root-count asymmetry has residual $0.089$ from the unit Born dipole and places $0.982$ of resolved odd power through $\ell=7$ in $\ell=1$. Across $N=11,\ldots,16$, the median polar score over four deterministic saved times rises monotonically from $0.192$ to $0.807$, while all 24 matched rows retain unit polar coverage and satisfy the declared second-harmonic gate. These observations establish a finite structured-versus-Haar contrast, not a thermodynamic Born law. Algebraic multiplicity remains distinct from physical probability, the roots generally require different detector microstates, and instantaneous pole factorization does not establish stable records. The resulting advance is therefore a concrete classification problem: which unitary Hamiltonians generate the root geometry, preparation measure, and record structure required for measurement?
