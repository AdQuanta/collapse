## Paragraph 1: Establishing the Territory (Subsystem Arrows of Time)

Spontaneous emission is the paradigmatic subsystem arrow of time: a localized atomic excitation decays irreversibly into the electromagnetic continuum, yet the combined atom-field state evolves strictly unitarily according to the Wigner-Weisskopf Hamiltonian. Environmental decoherence and many-body thermalization exhibit the identical physical architecture [1–3]. In closed interacting quantum systems, the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH) explains how local observables relax toward thermal equilibrium while global quantum coherence is rigorously preserved [4, 5]. Similarly, decoherence theory and Quantum Darwinism account for the emergence of classical pointer states and the redundant dissemination of classical information throughout an environment [6–10]. Across modern physics, apparent macroscopic irreversibility and loss of local phase coherence are reconciled with fundamental time-reversal symmetry through global unitary dynamics.

## Paragraph 2: Identifying the Tension and Why the Gap Matters

Despite these advances, quantum measurement remains the unresolved fourth arrow of time (Fig. 1). While decoherence explains the rapid suppression of off-diagonal phase interference, it does not select a single, definite outcome from a coherent superposition. The foundational obstruction is linearity: if two orthogonal eigenstates of an observable evolve into distinct macroscopic detector records $|0\rangle|\mathcal{D}_0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle|\mathcal{D}_1\rangle$, an unrestricted superposition principle demands that an arbitrary linear combination $\alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$ must evolve into a macroscopic superposition $\alpha|0\rangle|\mathcal{D}_0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle|\mathcal{D}_1\rangle$ [11]. Standard quantum mechanics resolves this tension by postulating an external, non-unitary wave-function reduction governed by Born's rule [12]. Objective collapse models instead introduce fundamental stochastic, non-linear modifications to the Schrödinger equation [13–15]. However, the possibility that measurement outcomes might emerge naturally within purely unitary quantum mechanics—without modifying linear dynamics—remains a tantalizing and largely unexamined frontier.

## Paragraph 3: Announcing the Present Work ("Here We") and Unlocking Intuition

Here we formulate an exact, constructive restricted-state framework wherein definite-outcome boundary states under unitary evolution correspond to the projective roots of a detector-space matrix pencil. Rather than modifying unitary dynamics or positing non-linear collapse, we investigate a narrower hypothesis: that physically realized global product inputs might occupy a dynamically privileged set not closed under arbitrary linear addition. This approach formalizes and extends the "special state" intuition originally proposed by Schulman [16–18], providing an exact algebraic classification of states that disentangle under finite-time evolution. Writing the unitary propagator in $2\times 2$ detector-block form, initial qubit states that evolve to a definite detector pole are governed by a homogeneous matrix pencil $(\alpha C + \beta D)\eta = 0$. Unitarity imposes a profound and previously unrecognized geometric constraint: by Jacobi's complementary-minor duality, the outcome-$1$ roots are forced to be exact Bloch antipodes of the outcome-$0$ roots. Consequently, a regular detector of Hilbert space dimension $d$ generates exactly $d$ projective coordinates and their labelled partners on the Bloch sphere, fundamentally linking the algebraic spectrum of the detector to an antipodal outcome geometry.

## Paragraph 4: Announcing Principal Findings and Empirical Contrast

We then establish that the emergence of Born-like polar statistics ($\cos^2(\theta/2)$) is not a generic consequence of chaotic mixing, but a specific property of structured many-body interactions. First, we prove an exact null theorem: for Haar-random unitaries, the projective roots form a complex spherical ensemble with uniform one-point intensity, precluding any ensemble-selected measurement axis. In sharp contrast, we demonstrate that a clean, interacting many-body spin ring with collective transverse coupling develops a pronounced full-sphere Born dipole. At $N=16$ detector spins ($65,536$ roots), the equal-area root asymmetry achieves a count-weighted residual of $0.089$ from the ideal Born dipole, with $98.2\%$ of resolved odd power concentrated in the $\ell=1$ mode. Across system sizes $N=11$ to $16$, the four-time median polar score rises monotonically from $0.192$ to $0.807$ while maintaining unit full-sphere coverage and low azimuthal distortion. Finally, we establish the theoretical boundaries of this phenomenon: certifying an exact constructive Hamiltonian family whose folded spectrum analytically satisfies the Born criterion, while proving rigorous no-go theorems for simple commuting fields and non-normal operator pencils. These results convert the measurement problem into a concrete many-body classification program, demonstrating that if measurement is unitary, its statistics are written in the algebraic structure of the detector.
